Testimony

My journey to Christ has been a complex journey, full of twists and turns. Or at least I thought it was. It wasn't until around the time that I started Empowering David that I feel I truly came to know and accept who Christ is. I had always believed in God but I didn't understand the Gospel until 2003. During that time a broken relationship shattered the self-righteous image I had of myself and I began reading the Bible. I thought that I had become a Christian but in reality I had only adopted Christianity as another attribute. I ended up getting married to a fellow believer who had two kids. She wanted a leader and I had no problem stepping into that role. It was a role that I was not prepared for and today I realize that my lack of preparation was really a lack of surrender. I was constantly full of doubt and anxiety. I trusted my intuition over God's word which resulted in me becoming an emotional vacuum. We argued a lot, jumped around from Church to Church, and eventually fell away from the faith. Every time something didn't work out I got more and more bitter, ultimately ending up in a very dark place. I was almost constantly angry and dissatisfied. Our marriage eventually fell apart leading me on a journey of self-discovery. That journey would eventually come full circle.

The most important thing that happened at this time was that I finally took full responsibility for my own suffering. I didn't make excuses about the kind of woman I was with. I didn't blame the stepkids. I blamed myself. I was convinced that the world would be a better place without me and considered taking myself out of it. I remember feeling frustrated because I could figure out technical issues but I could never figure out my own mind. Then it hit me. I could figure out technical issues because I approach tech as if I know nothing at all. I gather a lot of data before diagnosing a problem or trying to fix it. When it came to life, I always felt that I had the answers. I needed to take a more curious approach to understanding my mind.

I came up with many, many tangible mental models to understand my mind and my emotions. The goal was to learn about the problem so that I could implement a fix. Perhaps the most beneficial was mindfulness meditation with a twist. Mindfulness meditation is the practice of focusing on a sense (like touch) and then calling out as many things that you can sense as you can. I focused on my emotions rather than a physical sense, and then began categorizing my thoughts and feelings according to colors and then eventually symbols. This method worked wonders at first but then I realized that no matter how much I focus on what I feel there's more to find. It reminded me of looking up at the sky at night. I'm quite literally looking into eternity with what I see being limited only by my own senses. Yet something about that felt very comforting. When I looked inward I felt the same feeling of eternity but I didn't feel comforted. I felt a sense of dread. I am not responsible for the universe but I am responsible for what is within me.

This revelation played a major role in helping me see the light. I looked at a mental model that I had designed and evolved over time to help me understand myself. I realized that Christianity was one category among many. That's the problem. I had to trust God with what is within in the same way that I trust Him with the universe. To do that, I had to feel as helpless when I look within as I do when I look at the stars. I began a conscious effort to give myself to God fully and completely. I finally realized that I had spent all of my time in an endless cycle of attempting to prepare myself to be close to God and falling short. Christ died so that we can be close to God. I now realize that being close to God is not my reward for finding a solution. Being close to Him is the solution.

Today I own a home with my wife Becky. While I have made a lot of improvements I certainly had my struggles and I'm very thankful both to and for her for sticking with me. She was able to make some sense of my struggles when she realized that my beahvior patterns and approach to issues mirrors that of somebody who is high functioning autistic. That framing allows me to understand myself better. Specifically when it comes to fixating on things that cause anxiety. At our wedding, I chose the Fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23 as my verse to be read. Only I knew the meaning at the time. It reminded me of how wrong I was in my previous marriage and it prevented me from feeling as if God was on "my side" when I was clearly in the wrong. Later, consideration of "You know a tree by it's fruit" (Matthew 7:16), made it clear to me that I would be right with God when these attributes came naturally and effortlessly to me. I'm very happy to say that I've been experiencing exactly that.
