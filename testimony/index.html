Testimony

I initially read the Bible and developed a surface-level understanding of the Gospel in 2004. What started as a reading of "The Complete Idiot's Guide To The Bible" lead to a full read of the King James Bible followed by a period of extreme commitment. I threw out all of my secular movies and exchanged them for Christian films. I started going to Church and sometimes attended classes and Bible studies four to five days a week. What I failed to realize at the time is that I was not committing myself to God. Rather, I was creating a "very good Christian" archetype that became the main character in my own narrative. I had reduced the Gospel to nothing more than an extra life in a video game. It was a band-aid loophole that allowed me to convince myself that I had solved the problem that prevented me from being who I was supposed to be. It was still all about me.

I eventually met a fellow believer. She has two children and we would go on to get married. My ego convinced me that I was going to be the best husband and father figure in the world. A time went on, any time things didn't go according to how I thought they should I saw it as a threat. I didn't understand it at the time but that's only because I wasn't willing to accept the truth. The truth was that I had never let go of my ego. If something happened within my household then I felt it reflected poorly on me. I developed a very bitter and deflective attitude, eventually falling away from my faith with my wife soon following. I took a role where I was supposed to lead a family closer to God and in the end I did the exact opposite. The marriage fell apart. Accepting that I was getting a divorce broke me and that is exactly what I needed.

The beginning phase of this acceptance was very difficult. For the first time in my entire life I had finally accepted that I was the problem. Suddenly the narrative of my life began to change. I thought about friendships and relationships in the past and everything had been reframed. I grew frustrated because I was unable to understand how I could be so good at technical troubleshooting and so terrible at troubleshooting my own mind. Then it hit me. When I troubleshoot tech I approach every issue as if I know nothing at all. Yet when I approach life I act as if I have all of the answers. If I approach life with the same curiosity that leads me to technical solutions, perhaps I could finally understand how my mind works and fix it.

This kicked off a 13 year effort involving journaling and all kinds of diagnostic strategies derived from my tech background. One of these efforts used a modified version of Mindfulness Meditation combined with a categorization system to sort my thoughts and emotions. The last iteration of this consisted of a mental model that divided my thoughts and emotions into eight categories. The most important message came at the end of this effort. I realized that I would never be able to finish this effort. Looking within began to give me the same sensation I have when I look into the night sky. It terrifies me knowing how small I am but it also comforts me. I cannot possibly manage the galaxy. I consider it to be a great day when I can manage my own home. Therefore, I do not even try. I just trust that God has it under control. What choice do I have?

THAT'S IT! As I stared at this mental model that I designed the issue I've always had was clear. Christianity was merely a category to me. Every other category is something that I had yet to surrender to God. They were parts of me that I was hanging on to and I needed to let go. It was as if my mind was a home and God occupied a single room. Previously, I would clean all of the other rooms before I would approach God. Instead, I should approach God knowing that this is His home, not mine. He cleanses the other rooms rather than me. As a result, He gets the glory and I get the benefit of lifted burdens. This would not be possible had it not been for Jesus' sacrifice.
